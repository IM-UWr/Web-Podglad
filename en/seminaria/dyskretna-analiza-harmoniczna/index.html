Local functional equations for polynomials and related topics

Takeyoshi Kogiso (Josai University)

Abstract

The functional equations for the Riemann zeta function have long been known to admit two classical proofs. One is based on the Mellin transform and an integral representation of the zeta function, while the other relies on Jacobi’s theta function. Although these approaches are quite different, they are in fact two manifestations of the same underlying principle: The Poisson summation formula and the self-duality of the Fourier transform. The main theme of this lecture is how this remarkable phenomenon extends to higher dimensions. A successful framework for such a generalization is provided by the theory of prehomogeneous vector spaces, introduced by Mikio Sato. In this talk, I will discuss local functional equations associated with these spaces, the polynomials that satisfy them, and their intimate connections with representation theory, harmonic analysis and number theory, and combinatorics.

Conditionally free conditional expectations and applications to free denoising

Abstract

We study distributions of polynomials in c-free random variables. In a first we provide recursions that allow to compute joint Boolean cumulants of conditionally free random variables. A Karlin-McGregor formula for coalescing random walks

Piotr Śniady (IMPAN)

Abstract

The Karlin-McGregor theorem expresses the probability that nnn random walks (or Brownian motions) reach specified positions without collision as an n×nn\times nn×n determinant. When particles coalesce upon meeting, this structure appears to break: after kkk mergers only n−kn−kn−k particles remain, destroying the square matrix. We restore the dimension using ghost particles. When two paths meet, one continues as heir while the other becomes a ghost—continuing independently, invisible to the physical system. The entity count (heirs plus ghosts) remains nnn, enabling a determinant formula.

Generalized entropy in molecular biology and non-linear growth model of SARS-CoV-2 variant succession

Monika Kurpas (Politechnika Śląska)

Abstract

Entropy measures are widely used in molecular biology to, among other, describe sequence variability, identify genetic polymorphisms and evolutionarily conserved sites, analyze and predict binding sites, distinguish coding from non-coding regions, and predict RNA and protein secondary structure. Although Shannon entropy is most frequently used, the generalized entropies, such for example, Tsallis entropy, offer alternative descriptions for systems with strong correlations or heavy tails.

The analysis of time evolution of the cumulative tails derived from site-frequency spectra (SFS) computed from SARS-CoV-2 genomic data leads us to the discovery of a characteristic pattern, which includes a discontinuity, that can be traced down to a number of mutations, preserving identity over a time interval. In addition, the discontinuity moves, with time, towards higher frequencies, eventually dominating the whole population. We present a nonlinear two-phase growth model whose analytical solution takes the form of a q-exponential function—analogous to the functions that arise from Tsallis-entropy maximization—and show that it accurately reproduces the shape of the SARS-CoV-2 cumulative tails.

Unital embeddings of C*-algebras that one can see

Piotr M. Hajac (IMPAN)

Abstract

Cuntz algebras OnO_nOn​, n>1n>1n>1, are celebrated examples of a separable infinite simple C*-algebra with a number of fascinating properties. On positive Jacobi matrices with compact inverses

Grzegorz Świderski (Politechnika Wrocławska)

Abstract

We consider positive Jacobi matrices JJJ with compact inverses and consequently with purely discrete spectra. A number of properties of the corresponding sequence of orthogonal polynomials is studied including the convergence of their zeros, the vague convergence of the zero counting measures and of Christoffel—Darboux kernels on the diagonal. They are obtained from canonical decompositions of “matricial circular systems” studied recently in the context of the Hilbert space realization of the asymptotic joint *-distributions of symmetric blocks of independent block-identically distributed Gaussian random matrices with respect to partial traces. As compared with those systems, matricial R-circular systems describe the asymptotic joint *-distributions of blocks rather than those of symmetric blocks.

Abstract

W czasie referatu przeanalizowane zostaną dwa klasyczne dowody twierdzenia Wignera: za pomocą metod kombinatorycznych, oraz z wykorzystaniem transformaty Stieltjesa. Oba rozumowania przeniesione zostaną na przypadek, gdy dana duża macierz losowa X nie jest hermitowsko-symetryczna, ale H-samosprzężona, tzn. macierz HX jest hermitowsko-symetryczna. Macierz H będzie postaci diag(d,1,…,1), gdzie liczba d może być ujemna. Przedyskutowane zostaną również ograniczenia w rozszerzaniu obu metod na inne przypadki macierzy H.
